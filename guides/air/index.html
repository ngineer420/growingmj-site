Air Through

How fast the air in the space is replaced — which quietly sets your temperature, your humidity and whether anything smells.

Air exchange is the loop that fixes the other three by accident. Pull fresh air through a space fast enough and the heat leaves, the humidity leaves, the stale pocket where spider mites start never forms, and the smell goes through the filter on its way out. Undersize it and you spend the rest of the grow fighting symptoms that all trace back to one number.

The number, and what wrecks it

CFM is a volume per minute, so it starts from the size of the space and how often you want to turn it over. The part that catches people is everything bolted to the fan afterwards: a carbon filter, a run of ducting, every bend in it, and a speed controller you will want to run below maximum for noise. Each of those takes a bite out of the rating on the box, and they compound.

Buy it once

An undersized exhaust fan is the hardest first-grow mistake to fix, because the fix is a second fan rather than a refund. Size the exhaust before you buy the tent, not after — the tent's port diameter constrains what you can bolt to it, and the ports are not all the same.

Air inside the tent is a different job

Exhaust moves air out. A clip fan moves it around, and the two are not interchangeable. Gentle constant movement across the canopy builds stem strength, keeps humidity from pooling in the leaf layer, and breaks up the still air where fungal problems start. It does nothing about heat.
