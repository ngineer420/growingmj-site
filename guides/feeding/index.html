Salt In Solution

How strong the feed actually is — on whichever of the three PPM scales your meter happens to use.

Fertilizer is dissolved salt, and the useful measurement is how much of it is in the water: electrical conductivity. Everything else in this loop — which medium you chose, whether the pH lets the plant take the nutrients up, what the leaves are telling you — is downstream of that one number and the plant's ability to reach it.

The calculator for this loop: EC to PPM Converter — The 500, 640 and 700 scales side by side, because they disagree by 40% and almost nobody says which one they mean.

The unit problem, which is not a small problem

EC is a real measurement in mS/cm. PPM is EC multiplied by a conversion factor, and there are three factors in common use — 500, 640 and 700. A feed chart written on one scale and a meter reading on another are 40% apart, which is the difference between a light feed and nutrient burn. Meters rarely print the factor on the display.

The medium decides how much slack you have

Soil is a buffer: it holds nutrients on exchange sites and releases them slowly, so a bad feed is diluted by everything already there. Coco coir and rockwool are inert. They hold what you just poured in and nothing else, which is why they are less forgiving and also why they respond faster when you get it right. Coir additionally arrives high in potassium and sodium and low in calcium, which is the real reason cal-mag is a default in coir rather than any nutrient-binding story.

pH gates everything above

A nutrient that is present but locked out by pH produces exactly the same leaf as a nutrient that is absent. That is why every sensible deficiency workflow checks pH before it names an element, and why a pH meter is the highest-value cheap tool in the room.
