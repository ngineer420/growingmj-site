The Plant Itself

The loops are what you control. This is what you are controlling them for — and what to read when the plant is the one telling you something.

Nobody walks into a room thinking “my EC is wrong”. They walk in and see a yellow leaf, or a seedling that has gone leggy, or a basil plant that is one tall stalk instead of a bush. This section is the work you do with your hands on the plant, and the reading you do when the plant has an opinion about your four loops.

The calculator for this loop: Deficiency Diagnosis — Five questions about what you can see, answered with a ranked shortlist rather than one confident guess — pH lockout checked first.

Symptoms first, numbers second

A symptom is an entry point, not a diagnosis. Yellowing between the veins of new growth and yellowing of the whole oldest leaf are different problems with different fixes, and both of them can be caused by a pH that is fine on paper. The value of working symptom-first is that it tells you which number is worth measuring next, instead of leaving you to measure all four.

The work that is not a measurement

Starting seeds, rooting cuttings, pinching a growing tip so a plant branches instead of stretching, cutting herbs in a way that produces more herbs — none of these has a calculator, and all of them are what the room is for. They also interact with the loops in ways worth knowing: training a canopy flat is how you spend the light you already have, and a humidity dome is a deliberate, temporary VPD of nearly zero.

Pests belong here too

Most indoor pest problems are conditions problems wearing an insect costume. Fungus gnats arrive because a surface stays damp; spider mites arrive because the air is still and dry. Treating the insect without changing the condition buys a few weeks.
