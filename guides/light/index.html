Light In

How much light the plant actually receives in a day, as opposed to how bright the room looks to you.

Light is the loop most indoor growers get wrong first, and the one where intuition is least useful. The human eye adapts across five orders of magnitude, so a windowsill that reads as “bright” can be delivering a tenth of what a lettuce wants and you will not see the difference standing in the room.

The calculator for this loop: DLI Calculator — PPFD and hours in, daily light integral out — and a reverse mode that tells you which of the two to change.

Why the herb garden guide sits under light: insufficient light, not watering, is what kills an indoor herb first — the leggy, pale basil problem is a light problem with a watering story attached to it.

What the number actually is

Not watts, which describe the fixture rather than the plant. Not lux, which is weighted for human vision and quietly discounts the red end plants use most. The number is DLI: the total photosynthetically active photons landing on a square metre over the whole photoperiod, in mol/m²/day. Two levers set it, and only two — intensity (PPFD) and hours. That is the entire equation, which is why one calculator covers it.

How this loop fails

Too little and the plant stretches: long internodes, pale leaves, a seedling that is tall and cannot hold itself up. That is the failure people misread as needing more fertilizer. Too much and you get bleaching at the top of the canopy, which looks like a deficiency and is not. Between those, the common quiet failure is a schedule that wanders — the same total hours delivered at different times each day.

The shopping consequence

Fixtures are sold on wattage because wattage is easy to print. What decides whether a light works is coverage: the PPFD map over the footprint your pots actually occupy. A fixture with no published map is an unknown quantity, and a fixture sized for a 4x4 tent is the wrong answer for a shelf of greens by a factor of ten.
