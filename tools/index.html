Grow Room Tools

Four numbers that decide whether an indoor grow works, worked out for you — and one tool for when you have no number yet, only a plant that looks wrong.

Every one of these runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and there is no sign-up — once the page has loaded it keeps working with the phone in aeroplane mode, which is the usual state of a phone standing in a tent. More importantly, each one names the convention it uses. Grow calculators disagree with each other constantly, and it is almost never because one is broken; it is because one silently used air temperature where another used leaf temperature, or the 500 scale where your meter uses 700. Those choices are exposed here as inputs you control rather than assumptions buried in the code.

Why These Five

An indoor grow is four control loops wearing a trench coat. Light in (DLI), water out (VPD), air through (CFM), and salt in solution (EC). Nearly every problem a beginner posts a photo of is one of those four being out of range, and nearly every one of them is invisible without a number: a tent can look and smell perfect at 0.4 kPa while the plants quietly stop drinking.

The fifth tool exists because that is not how anyone actually arrives. Nobody walks into a tent thinking “my EC is wrong”; they walk in and see a yellow leaf. The deficiency diagnoser takes that entry point and turns it into a measurement worth making, which usually means sending you to one of the other four with a specific question instead of a vague worry. It is also the only one that will tell you what it has ruled out, and the only one that answers in bands rather than a number — because five questions cannot support a percentage.

The other reason to run the numbers is money. Two of these — the fan calculator and the DLI calculator — are shopping tools. Buying a fan that turns out to be 40% too small once a carbon filter is bolted to it is one of the most common and most expensive first-grow mistakes, and the fix is a second fan, not a refund.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do these grow calculators need an account or an internet connection?

No. Every calculator runs entirely in your browser with no account, no sign-up and no data sent anywhere. Once the page has loaded it keeps working offline, so a phone in a tent with no signal still gets an answer.

Which calculator should I use first?

If something already looks wrong on the plant, start with the deficiency diagnoser — it is the only one here that takes symptoms rather than numbers, and it checks pH lockout before it will name a nutrient. If the plants look fine and you are tuning the room, start with VPD, the single number that ties your temperature and humidity together. If you are still buying gear, start with the fan calculator, because an undersized exhaust fan is the hardest mistake to fix later.

Why do different websites give different answers for the same inputs?

Because they quietly use different conventions. VPD changes depending on whether leaf or air temperature is assumed, and by how much. EC to PPM changes by 40% depending on whether the meter uses the 500, 640 or 700 scale. CFM changes depending on which derates are baked in. Every calculator here names the convention it uses and lets you change it.

Are these calculators only for cannabis?

No. VPD, DLI, EC and air exchange are plant physiology and building physics, not crop-specific tricks. The same numbers apply to lettuce, basil, tomatoes, orchids and seedlings — only the target ranges change, and each page lists targets for several crops.
